1. Summary

Overall the home is in serviceable condition for its age and has been maintained. Three safety items need attention before or shortly after possession. The furnace is beyond its typical service life and should be budgeted for. Grading and downspout issues at the rear are the most likely source of future basement moisture and are inexpensive to correct. All findings are detailed in the sections that follow.

How to read this report: Safety items present a risk to occupants and should be corrected promptly. Repair items are defects that need correction to prevent further damage or restore function. Monitor items are conditions or components near end of life to budget for. Maintenance items are routine upkeep. Cost ranges are rough guides for planning only; obtain quotes from licensed contractors.

2. Roof

Method: walked. Covering: 3-tab asphalt shingles, one layer, approx. 2016. Flashing: metal at chimney and sidewalls. Ventilation: soffit intake, 4 roof vents, 1 ridge section.

Photo: north slope, moss growth

2.1 Moss on north slope Monitor

Light moss growth on the north-facing slope, shaded by the neighbour's maple. Shingles otherwise lie flat with good granule coverage. Expected remaining life 10 to 15 years.

Recommend: zinc strip at the ridge or a moss treatment; avoid pressure washing. Trim overhanging branches. Est. $150 to $400.
Photo: rear chimney, lifted counter-flashing

2.2 Deteriorated flashing at rear chimney Repair

Counter-flashing on the uphill side of the rear chimney has separated from the mortar joint and is sealed with caulking that has cracked. Staining on the attic sheathing directly below confirms occasional water entry (see 9.4).

Recommend: a roofer re-let and seal the counter-flashing. Est. $300 to $600.
Photo: eavestrough, rear

2.3 Eavestroughs partly clogged Maintenance

Debris in rear eavestroughs; front is clear. Sloped correctly, no visible separation.

Recommend: clean twice a year. Consider gutter guards on the tree side.

3. Exterior and grading

Photo: rear, grade sloping toward wall

3.1 Negative grading at rear Repair

Soil along the rear foundation wall has settled and slopes toward the house for approx. 6 m under the deck. Combined with 3.2, this directs roof water at the foundation. Efflorescence on the inside of this wall (4.2) is consistent with this.

Recommend: regrade with clay-based fill to slope away 150 mm over the first 2 m. Est. $400 to $1,200.
Photo: downspout, rear right corner

3.2 Downspouts discharge at foundation Repair

Two of four downspouts terminate at the foundation without extensions. The rear right downspout empties directly onto the area in 3.1.

Recommend: add extensions to discharge at least 1.8 m from the foundation, or connect to a splash block on grade sloping away. Est. under $100 DIY.
Photo: brick, west elevation

3.3 Brick veneer and mortar Maintenance

Brick in good condition. Minor mortar erosion at the west elevation below the kitchen window. Weep holes present and clear at base course. Caulking at brick-to-window joints is aged and beginning to crack at several locations.

Recommend: re-caulk window perimeters this season. Spot tuck-pointing below kitchen window within 2 to 3 years.
Photo: rear deck, ledger

3.4 Deck Maintenance

Pressure-treated deck, approx. 2010, ledger lag-bolted to rim joist with flashing present. Railings secure, 36" height. Several boards cupped and one stair tread cracked. Finish weathered.

Recommend: replace cracked tread, clean and re-seal.

4. Structure and basement

Photo: NW corner crack

4.1 Hairline vertical crack, NW corner Monitor

Vertical shrinkage-type crack approx. 1 mm wide, 1.2 m long, in the poured foundation wall near the NW corner. No displacement, no staining, dry at time of inspection. Typical for poured concrete of this age.

Recommend: mark the ends and monitor. If it widens or shows moisture, have it injection-sealed from inside (est. $400 to $700).
Photo: rear wall, behind finished wall opening

4.2 Efflorescence on rear foundation wall Monitor

White mineral deposits visible on the unfinished portion of the rear wall in the utility room. Indicates moisture has moved through the concrete in the past. Moisture meter readings normal today. Corresponds with the exterior conditions in 3.1 and 3.2.

Recommend: correct exterior grading and downspouts first, then monitor. No interior waterproofing needed at this time.

Framing: 2x10 floor joists at 16" o.c. with plywood subfloor, visible in utility room, no notching, sagging or moisture damage observed. Steel beam on adjustable steel posts, plumb. Basement finished approx. 2012; finished areas limit observation of foundation walls to roughly 30%.

5. Electrical

Service: 100 A, 240 V, underground. Panel: Square D QO, 30 circuits, located in utility room, labelled, no double-taps, no scorching. Branch wiring: copper NMD90. Grounding: to water main and ground rod.

Photo: kitchen counter receptacle, tester

5.2 No GFCI protection at kitchen counter receptacles Safety

The two receptacles within 1.5 m of the sink are not GFCI protected and are not on a GFCI breaker. Bathroom and exterior receptacles are protected.

Recommend: licensed electrician install GFCI receptacles or a GFCI breaker. Est. $150 to $300.
Photo: second floor hallway

5.5 No carbon monoxide alarm near second-floor bedrooms Safety

CO alarm present on the main floor only. A home with fuel-burning appliances and an attached garage should have CO alarms adjacent to all sleeping areas. Smoke alarms present on each level; the basement unit is past its 10-year date stamp.

Recommend: install a CO alarm in the second-floor hallway and replace the basement smoke alarm. Under $100.

Sampled: 38 receptacles tested, 2 with reversed polarity in the basement bedroom (repair, est. $100), all others correct. Exterior receptacles GFCI protected with covers. Garage receptacle on GFCI.

6. Plumbing

Supply: 3/4" copper main, copper distribution, main shut-off at front wall, operable. Drains: ABS, ventilated. Water pressure: 60 psi at exterior bib. Functional flow: good with two fixtures running.

Photo: under kitchen sink

6.2 Leak at hot water shut-off under kitchen sink Repair

Slow drip at the packing nut of the hot-side angle stop. Cabinet floor is stained and slightly swollen at the back. Moisture meter reads elevated at the cabinet base.

Recommend: replace angle stop, dry and inspect cabinet floor. Est. $100 to $200 plumber, less DIY.
Photo: water heater label

6.4 Water heater is a rental Monitor

50-gallon gas water heater, approx. 2020, rental tag from a utility. Vent and gas connections appear correct. Temperature/pressure relief valve present with discharge pipe.

Recommend: confirm the rental contract terms and monthly cost with the seller; ensure it's disclosed in the Agreement of Purchase and Sale.

Sump: pit with pump present in utility room, discharges to rear yard, ran when float lifted. No backwater valve visible; recommend confirming with the municipality whether one exists, and consider adding one (est. $1,500 to $3,000, often subsidized). Fixtures: all bathroom and kitchen fixtures operated. Toilet on second floor is loose at the floor (maintenance, re-set wax seal).

7. Heating and cooling

Photo: furnace data plate

7.1 Furnace near end of typical service life Monitor

Mid-efficiency gas forced-air furnace, approx. 2004, 80,000 BTU. Operated normally under thermostat control; burners lit evenly, blower and inducer normal, no unusual noise. Flame roll-out and heat exchanger not visible without disassembly. Typical service life is 15 to 25 years; this unit is at 22.

Recommend: budget for replacement with a high-efficiency unit within 1 to 3 years (est. $4,500 to $7,000). Have it serviced annually; ask the seller for service records.

Cooling: central A/C, approx. 2019, 2.5 ton. Operated; 16°C temperature drop across the coil, normal. Condenser level, fins clean, refrigerant line insulated. Distribution: supply and return in every room; several basement supply boots not sealed to the floor (maintenance). Filter dirty, replace.

8. Interior

Photo: family room window, fogging

8.1 Failed seal, family room window; original second-floor windows Repair Monitor

Condensation between panes on the family room picture window (seal failure; cosmetic, reduces efficiency). Main floor windows replaced approx. 2015; second floor are original 1994 double-glazed vinyl, operable, weather stripping worn.

Recommend: replace the sealed unit in the family room (est. $400 to $700). Budget for second-floor windows in 5 to 10 years.
Photo: basement stair railing

8.3 Loose railing at basement stairs Safety

Wall-mounted handrail moves approx. 25 mm under hand pressure; one bracket screwed into drywall only.

Recommend: re-secure brackets into studs or blocking. Under $50.

Walls and ceilings: minor settlement cracks above two door openings, typical. No staining on ceilings. Floors: level within normal tolerance; squeak at upper hallway. Kitchen and bathrooms: caulking at tub surround in main bath failing, re-caulk (maintenance). Exhaust fans operate; see 9.3. Doors: bedroom 3 door rubs at the latch side, plane or adjust.

9. Attic, insulation and ventilation

Access: hatch in upper hallway, entered. Insulation: blown cellulose, approx. 400 mm (about R-50), evenly distributed, baffles at eaves. Ventilation: adequate; soffits open. Sheathing: plywood, sound.

Photo: dryer vent hood, exterior

9.3 Dryer vent damper stuck open, lint build-up Repair

Exterior dryer vent damper is held open by lint. The duct inside the wall could not be viewed. Bathroom exhaust fans are ducted to the roof vents (correct).

Recommend: clean the dryer duct end to end and replace the hood. Est. $100 to $200. Clean annually.
Photo: sheathing stain below rear chimney

9.4 Staining on sheathing below rear chimney Monitor

Dark staining, dry today, on the sheathing and one rafter below the rear chimney, consistent with the flashing condition in 2.2. No soft wood, no active drip.

Recommend: correct 2.2, then re-check after the next heavy rain.

10. Garage

Photo: opener test, 2x4 under door

10.1 Garage door opener auto-reverse not functioning Repair

The overhead door did not reverse when closing on a 2x4 laid on the floor. Photo-eye sensors present and working; the force setting is the issue.

Recommend: adjust the down-force on the opener per the manual, re-test. If it fails, service the opener. Under $150.

Fire separation between garage and house intact; door to house is solid-core with self-closer. Slab in good condition with minor shrinkage cracks. Two receptacles on GFCI.

12. Scope and limitations

This inspection was performed in accordance with a published Standards of Practice for home inspectors and is a visual, non-invasive examination of the readily accessible systems and components of the home on the date noted. It is not a code compliance inspection, a warranty, or a guarantee, and it does not identify concealed conditions. Areas not inspected due to access, occupancy or conditions: approx. 70% of foundation walls (finished basement), roof underside at the front (insulation depth), crawlspace under the front porch (no access), interior of ductwork, and the condition of the heat exchanger. Furniture and stored items were not moved. Refer to the inspection agreement for full terms.

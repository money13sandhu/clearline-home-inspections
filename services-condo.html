Condo inspection

"It's a condo, the building takes care of everything" is how people end up paying for a failed in-suite heat pump, a leaking window frame, or a bathroom fan that vents into the ceiling.

What's inspected inside the unit

  • Electrical: panel, breakers, every accessible outlet and switch, GFCI protection, smoke and CO alarms.
  • Plumbing: supply and drain materials (Kitec is common in 1995 to 2007 GTA condos and is flagged), shut-off valves, fixtures, water pressure, leaks under sinks, water heater or in-suite tank, laundry connections.
  • Heating and cooling: fan coil or heat pump operation, filter, condensate drain, thermostat, signs of past leaks around the unit.
  • Windows and doors: operation, seals, condensation between panes, balcony door, entry door and hardware.
  • Moisture: thermal scan and moisture meter at exterior walls, below windows, around tubs and showers, and at the ceiling below any unit above.
  • Ventilation: bathroom and kitchen exhaust actually moving air; dryer venting.
  • Interior finishes: flooring, walls, ceilings, cabinets, countertops, appliances operated on a basic cycle.
  • Balcony and locker/parking: surface, railing, drainage, door threshold; parking spot and locker visually checked when access is provided.

What we look at in the building

Common elements are the corporation's responsibility, but they're also your money through maintenance fees and special assessments. We note visible concerns in the hallways, garage, mechanical spaces and exterior that you should ask about in the status certificate review: efflorescence or spalling in the parking garage, staining at the building envelope, elevator or fire safety notices, and the general condition of shared systems.

Status certificate. We're happy to read the engineering and reserve fund sections alongside your lawyer's review and flag anything that lines up with what we saw on site.

How long does it take?

About 1.5 to 2 hours for a typical one- or two-bedroom unit. Larger units, townhouse-style condos and older buildings take longer. Report within 24 hours, usually the same evening.
